# Writing Effective Rubrics

The quality of student feedback generated by Feedback Kitchen depends directly on the care taken when authoring rubric descriptors. A well-constructed rubric produces clear, actionable feedback that students can apply to future assessments.

### The two essential rules

Two core authoring rules distinguish effective Feedback Kitchen rubrics:

1. **Write in the second person:** Address the student directly (*"Your analysis moves beyond description..."*, not *"The student showed good understanding"*). The assembled text is delivered straight to the student as personal commentary.
2. **Ensure every descriptor is self-contained:** In traditional grid rubrics, students read across the columns to understand what separated a B from an A. In Feedback Kitchen, students see only the single descriptor matching their awarded grade. The statement must make complete, independent sense without reference to neighboring tiers.

### Calibrating the five performance tiers

Each assessment criterion defines descriptors across five performance tiers. The NZ preset uses four of them and leaves satisfactory unused; every other preset uses all five.

| Tier | Pedagogical Focus | Descriptor Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Excellent** | Mastery & Distinction | Identifies what distinguishes top-tier execution. Avoids generic praise (*"Great work"*); highlights analytical depth, synthesis, or original application. |
| **Proficient** | Competence & Standard | Confirms that all core requirements and learning outcomes are fully and securely met. |
| **Developing** | Targeted Gap Identification | Names the specific gap or missing component and points toward the next level of achievement. |
| **Satisfactory** | Minimum Standard Met | Confirms the work clears the threshold while naming what keeps it there. Unused by the NZ preset. |
| **Unsatisfactory** | Foundational Support | Identifies major conceptual omissions without harshness, providing a clear starting point for academic remediation. |

### Selecting criteria count and weights

- **Target four to six criteria:** Four to six criteria provide sufficient diagnostic breadth without overwhelming the student. A rubric with twelve criteria creates grading fatigue and generates repetitive feedback checklists.
- **Distribute weights purposefully:** Assign higher percentage weights (e.g. 30–40%) to primary analytical outcomes, and lower weights (e.g. 10–15%) to mechanics such as formatting or referencing.

### Framing intro and outro templates

Introductory and closing templates provide tone and longitudinal framing:

- **Intro templates:** Acknowledge submission effort and establish the overall evaluative tone corresponding to the student's grade level.
- **Outro templates:** Focus on forward-looking academic advice, directing top-performing students toward publication or extension, and guiding lower-scoring students toward office hours and study support services.
